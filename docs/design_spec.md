# Design specification: simulation study of power under enrollment, dropout and delayed effects

**Status:** DRAFT for review (Checkpoint 2). No scenario has been run.
**Planning inputs:** `results/control_model.rds`, `results/stage1_summary.rds` (from `scripts/01_explore.R`)
and `results/design_calcs.rds` (from `scripts/02_design_calcs.R`). Every number below that comes from
data or planning calculations can be regenerated from those files.

---

## 1. Question

How do the enrollment rate, dropout and a delayed treatment effect change the power of a two-arm,
event-driven overall-survival (OS) trial? Does analyzing with a Fleming–Harrington FH(0,1) weighted
log-rank test, instead of the standard log-rank test, change which design we would recommend?

## 2. Data-generating model

### 2.1 Time scale and origin
- All times are in **months** (1 month = 365.25/12 days).
- *Calendar time* starts at study activation (t = 0). *Patient time* starts at each patient's
  randomization.

### 2.2 Control arm: piecewise-exponential (PWE) hazard
The control arm is calibrated to the Obs arm of `survival::colon` (OS, `etype == 2`, n = 315, 168 deaths)
by maximum likelihood (deaths ÷ person-time per interval):

| Patient time (months) | 0–6 | 6–12 | 12–24 | 24–48 | ≥ 48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hazard (per month) | 0.00265 | 0.01060 | 0.01610 | 0.01260 | 0.00646 |
| Hazard (per year) | 0.032 | 0.127 | 0.194 | 0.151 | 0.077 |

**Why PWE rather than Weibull:** the empirical hazard rises to a peak in months 12–24 and then falls.
A Weibull hazard is monotone and cannot fit that shape. The PWE has the lower AIC
(1854.0 vs 1881.1) and stays within 0.020 of the KM curve everywhere, versus 0.082 for the Weibull.
Fitting the early part of the hazard well matters here, because that is the period a delayed effect
acts on.

### 2.3 Treatment effect patterns, HR(t) relative to control (t = patient time)

| Label | Pattern | HR(t) |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Null | 1 |
| (b) | Proportional hazards (PH) | 0.69 for all t |
| (c) | 6-month delay | 1 for t < 6; 0.69 for t ≥ 6 |
| (d) | 12-month delay | 1 for t < 12; 0.69 for t ≥ 12 |

**Why HR 0.69:** this is the Cox estimate for Lev+5FU vs Obs in `colon` (0.689, 95% CI 0.546–0.869),
rounded to two decimals. `cox.zph` shows no evidence against PH (p = 0.28). The delayed-effect patterns
are **hypothetical**, motivated by immunotherapy trials. The colon data do not ground them. The
period-specific HR in the first 12 months (1.08, 95% CI 0.62–1.90) is far too imprecise to tell a delay
apart from PH.

The treatment-arm hazard is the control PWE hazard × HR(t). It is still piecewise constant, with
breakpoints {0, 6, 12, 24, 48} ∪ {delay}.

**Note on the control hazard:** because the control hazard in months 0–6 is low (0.032/yr), a 6-month
delay removes very few events from the comparison. A 12-month delay affects more. This is a property of
the adjuvant setting, and the report will discuss it.

### 2.4 Enrollment
- Poisson arrivals at a constant rate r ∈ {15, 30} patients/month. Inter-arrival times are
  i.i.d. Exponential(r), so entry time e_i = sum of the first i gaps.
- Enrollment stops when N patients are enrolled or the analysis is triggered, whichever comes first.
- **Allocation:** exactly N/2 per arm, assigned by a random permutation of the N slots in enrollment
  order. Every N in the grid is even.

### 2.5 Event and dropout times
- **Death time** T_i is generated by inversion. Draw E ~ Exponential(1) and set T_i = H⁻¹(E), where H is
  the arm's piecewise-linear cumulative hazard. This is exact for a PWE.
- **Dropout time** L_i ~ Exponential(δ), independent of arm and of T_i. Here δ = −log(1 − p)/12 for an
  annual dropout probability p ∈ {0, 0.05, 0.15}. "5% per year" means a 5% chance of dropping out
  within a year if the patient doesn't die.
- A patient's death is observable only if T_i < L_i.

## 3. Analysis timing (data cut)

- **Target deaths:** D = **306**. This is Schoenfeld's formula, 4(z₀.₉₇₅ + z₀.₉₀)² / (log 0.69)² = 305.3,
  rounded up. It gives 90% power for a one-sided α = 0.025 log-rank test under PH with 1:1 allocation.
- **Cap:** C = **84 months** of calendar time after study activation.
  - Median follow-up in `colon` was 77 months, and the PWE beyond 48 months is estimated from 31 deaths.
    So 84 months (7 years) is about the longest horizon the calibration supports.
  - It is also a realistic upper limit for an adjuvant OS trial.
- **Analysis time:** t* = min(calendar time of the D-th observed death, C).
- **Trigger** is recorded as `events` if the D-th death comes first and `cap` otherwise.
- **At the cut,** only patients with e_i ≤ t* are included. Each patient's follow-up is
  min(T_i, L_i, t* − e_i), and the status is 1 if T_i ≤ min(L_i, t* − e_i). When the trigger is
  `events`, exactly D deaths are analyzed (event times are continuous, so there are no ties).

Planning calculation (analytic expected deaths, not simulation), for the base-case neighborhood
(6-month delay, 5%/yr dropout):

| N | Accrual, 30/mo (months) | Expected time to D, 30/mo | Expected time to D, 15/mo |
|---|---|---|---|
| 600 | 20.0 | 149.8 (cap binds) | 160.2 (cap binds) |
| 700 | 23.3 | 100.4 (cap binds) | 112.7 (cap binds) |
| 800 | 26.7 | 75.7 | 91.1 (cap binds) |
| 900 | 30.0 | 64.1 | 82.9 |
| 1000 | 33.3 | 58.7 | 79.8 |
| 1200 | 40.0 | 54.1 | 79.0 |

At 15/month, no N reaches D in under about 79 months, so the trial is mainly limited by the cap.

## 4. Scenario grid

| Factor | Levels |
|---|---|
| Effect pattern | (a) null, (b) PH, (c) 6-mo delay, (d) 12-mo delay |
| N | 400, 500, 600, 700, 800, 900, 1000, 1200 |
| Enrollment rate (per month) | 15, 30 |
| Annual dropout | 0, 0.05, 0.15 |

That is 4 × 8 × 2 × 3 = **192 scenarios**: 48 null and 144 non-null.

**Why this N range (changed from the default 400/600/800):** in an event-driven design, power under PH
depends mainly on the number of deaths analyzed, not on N. N determines how quickly D accrues, and
therefore whether the cap triggers first and how many deaths are then available.
- The planning approximation (Schoenfeld power at the expected deaths analyzed, PH) ranges from
  **0.54** (N = 400, 15%/yr dropout) to **0.90** (D reached before the cap).
- Power can't go much above 0.90, because D was sized for 90% power. The 95% end of the requested
  "50–95%" range is not reachable without changing D. Extending N to 1200 shows where the duration
  levels off.

## 5. Methods (applied to the same simulated dataset in every replicate)

Let t₁ < … < t_K be the distinct death times in the pooled data at the cut. At each t_j:
- n_j and d_j are the number at risk and the number of deaths.
- n₁ⱼ and d₁ⱼ are the same counts in the treatment arm.

Weighted log-rank statistic:

U = Σ_j w_j (d₁ⱼ − d_j n₁ⱼ / n_j),
V = Σ_j w_j² d_j (n₁ⱼ/n_j)(1 − n₁ⱼ/n_j)(n_j − d_j)/(n_j − 1),
Z = U / √V.

Negative Z means fewer treatment deaths than expected, which favors treatment.
The **one-sided p-value** is Φ(Z).

1. **Log-rank:** w_j = 1.
2. **FH(0,1):** w_j = 1 − Ŝ(t_j−), where Ŝ is the **pooled** Kaplan–Meier estimate, taken just before
   t_j. This weight starts at 0 and grows as pooled survival falls, so it up-weights late differences.

Both are implemented in our own code and validated in Stage 3:
- log-rank against `survival::survdiff`
- FH(0,1) against `nphRCT` and/or `simtrial`

**Cox model:** `coxph(Surv(time, status) ~ trt)` with Efron ties (the default). Record the log HR, its
SE and the 95% Wald CI.

**Success:** one-sided p < 0.025 in favor of treatment.

## 6. Operating characteristics (per scenario, per method where relevant)

| Quantity | Definition | MCSE |
|---|---|---|
| Power / type I error | Proportion with p < 0.025 | √(p̂(1 − p̂)/R) |
| Paired power difference (FH − LR) | p̂_FH − p̂_LR | √((p₁₀ + p₀₁ − (p₁₀ − p₀₁)²)/R), where p₁₀ and p₀₁ are the proportions of discordant replicates |
| Cox log HR: mean, empirical SD | Over replicates | SD/√R for the mean |
| Mean 95% CI width of log HR | Mean of 2 × 1.96 × SE | SD of widths/√R |
| Bias, coverage (**PH scenario (b) only**) | Mean log HR − log 0.69; proportion of CIs containing log 0.69 | SD/√R; √(ĉ(1 − ĉ)/R) |
| Expected study duration | Mean t* | SD(t*)/√R |
| % hitting the cap | Proportion with trigger = cap | Binomial |
| Deaths analyzed, patients enrolled at the cut | Mean | SD/√R |

Under non-PH patterns the Cox HR is a time-averaged quantity that depends on follow-up. We report its
mean and SD, but no bias or coverage.

## 7. Replicates and Monte Carlo error

- **Non-null scenarios: R = 2,000.** The MCSE of power is ≤ 0.0112 (worst case, p = 0.5) and 0.0089 at
  p = 0.80. So ±2 MCSE ≈ ±1.8 percentage points around the 80% threshold.
- **Null scenarios: R = 10,000.** The MCSE of type I error at 0.025 is 0.00156. So the eligibility
  threshold is 0.025 + 2 × 0.00156 = **0.0281**.
- **Total:** 48 × 10,000 + 144 × 2,000 = 768,000 simulated trials. Stage 3 will project the runtime and
  propose a trimmed grid if it exceeds about an hour.

## 8. Recommendation rule (pre-specified)

A **design** is a pair (N, method). D = 306 and C = 84 months are fixed.

1. **Eligibility.** A design is eligible if its type I error is ≤ 0.0281 in **every null scenario with
   the same N**. That means all 6 combinations of enrollment (15, 30) × dropout (0, 5, 15%).
   - *Known property:* even if the true type I error were exactly 0.025, each scenario has about a 2.3%
     chance of exceeding the threshold. So there's about a 13% chance that a valid design is excluded
     by chance. If this happens, it will be reported, but the rule stands.
2. **Base case:** pattern (c) 6-month delay, **30 patients/month**, 5%/yr dropout.
   - *Why 30 rather than 15/month:* at 15/month, the expected time to D is at least about 79 months for
     every N (Section 3). No design could meet the duration limit below, so the event-driven design
     would really be a calendar-time design. The 15/month level stays in the grid as a sensitivity
     factor.
3. **Selection.** Among eligible designs, choose the **smallest N** for which at least one method
   reaches both of these in the base case:
   - simulated power ≥ 0.80 (point estimate)
   - expected study duration ≤ **72 months** (6 years)

   The duration limit is the planning target. The 84-month cap is only a backstop.
4. **Method at that N.**
   - If only one method qualifies, choose it.
   - If both qualify, choose **log-rank** unless FH(0,1) has higher power by more than 2 paired MCSEs.
     Log-rank is the default because it is the standard analysis and is efficient under PH.
5. **If no design qualifies,** report that, and report the design that comes closest, as a finding.

### Sensitivity (pre-specified)
Starting from the base case, change one factor at a time:
- enrollment 15/month
- dropout 0% or 15%/yr
- pattern (b) PH or (d) 12-month delay

The recommended design is **sensitive** to a factor level if any of these hold at that level:
- (i) its power falls below 0.80
- (ii) the other method's power is higher by more than 2 paired MCSEs
- (iii) its expected duration exceeds 72 months

For each level, we also report which design the rule would have chosen.

## 9. Reproducibility

- **Seeds:**
  - master seed `20261029`
  - scenario k (1–192, in the grid order above) uses `furrr_options(seed = 20261029 + k)`
  - furrr then gives each replicate its own L'Ecuyer-CMRG stream, so results don't depend on the number
    of workers
- **Saved outputs:**
  - per-replicate results in `results/` (Z, p and reject for each method; Cox log HR and SE; t*;
    trigger; deaths; patients enrolled)
  - summaries recomputed from those files
  - `sessionInfo()` and seeds logged
- **Packages:** locked with `renv` (R 4.5.1). Simulation uses base R + `survival`; parallelism uses
  `future`/`furrr`.

## 10. Out of scope
Interim analyses, MaxCombo or other tests, RMST, covariate adjustment and Bayesian methods are out of
scope. The report mentions them only as extensions.
